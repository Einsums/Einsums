=============================================
einsums::compute_graph::contribution_symmetry
=============================================

.. note::
   Generated from the C++ headers by ``apiary --emit-cpp-docs-json``.

Defined in header ``Einsums/ComputeGraph/SymmetryRules.hpp``.

.. cpp:namespace:: einsums::compute_graph

.. cpp:function:: std::optional<SymmetryOp> contribution_symmetry(const EscapeAnalysis &escape, NodeId writer, const SymmetryOp &sigma, std::vector<std::pair<TensorId, SymmetryDescriptor>> *relied_on)

   Is what ``writer`` adds to its output unchanged, up to a sign, when its output's axes are permuted by ``sigma``?

   The question a pass asks before computing half of a contraction and permuting the rest from it.
   It is asked of the CONTRIBUTION, the term ``writer`` adds, not of the output tensor, which may
   hold anything else as well.

   An operand of ``writer`` that exactly one einsum writes, by overwriting it, is replaced by that
   einsum's own operands, and so on down, so a chain of contractions is judged as the one
   expression it computes. The density-fitted particle ladder, ``H`` = B tau then ``R`` += H B, is
   judged as ``R`` += B tau B. The remaining factors are then matched against their own images: the
   contribution is unchanged when some relabeling of the summed letters, together with ``sigma`` on
   the output letters, carries every factor onto a factor of the same tensor, up to an element of
   that tensor's symmetry group as it stands where it is read (``symmetry_at``). On the ladder the
   relabeling exchanges e and f, the two B factors trade places, and tau's (i,a)-(j,b) exchange
   absorbs the rest.

   Proof, not evidence: every step is an identity the operands satisfy, so a positive answer holds
   for all values. A negative one may be conservative: the search takes generators as declared and
   gives up on anything conjugated.

   :param escape: The analysis of the graph ``writer`` belongs to.
   :param writer: An einsum node.
   :param sigma: The permutation of the output's positions, in the convention of ``SymmetryOp``: position ``p`` takes the letter at ``sigma.permutation[p]``. Its sign and conjugate fields are ignored.
   :param relied_on: When given, receives the declared symmetries the proof rests on, as ``symmetry_at`` reports them, each tensor once.
   :returns: ``sigma`` with the sign the contribution picks up (+1 symmetric, -1 antisymmetric), or nothing when no proof was found.
